    Check it in person

    Leaflet base
    One or two small teeth near the base of each leaflet, each with a gland underneath. Sumac and walnut leaflets are toothed along the whole edge.
    Smell
    Crushed leaves and twigs smell like burnt peanut butter or rancid nuts.
    Leaves
    Huge pinnate leaves, 1–4 ft long, with 11–41 leaflets arranged alternately on the twig.
    Bark and seeds
    Pale grey bark with light streaks, like cantaloupe skin. Clusters of papery, twisted seeds that turn red-brown in late summer.

    Tree of heaven came from China as an ornamental in the late 1700s. It forms dense thickets that crowd out native plants, damages pavement and foundations, and is the preferred host of the spotted lanternfly.

    1. Make sure

      Look for the glandular teeth at the base of each leaflet and the burnt peanut butter smell (see Check a photo). Sumac, black walnut and native ash are often removed by mistake.

    2. Look for spotted lanternflies

      Check the trunk for grey, mud-like egg masses and for spotted nymphs or adults. Lanternfly is a regulated pest: report it to your state department of agriculture.

    3. Report it

      Press Report to my state on any scan result or photo check. It drafts an email with the location and photo link, addressed to your state’s agency when it collects these reports. You can also add the tree to EDDMapS, the reporting map linked from the USDA’s invasive species site, or post it on iNaturalist. Either helps land managers see where it is spreading.

    4. Remove it the right way

      Seedlings and small saplings: pull them by hand, ideally when the soil is moist, and get the whole root.

      Bigger trees: don't just cut them down. A cut tree sprouts vigorously from the stump and roots, and you end up with a thicket. Foresters treat the standing tree with herbicide, either cuts in the trunk treated straight away (“hack and squirt”) or a band on the lower bark (“basal bark”). Late summer to fall works best for most methods. Mass Audubon suggests basal bark treatment in late winter or early spring. Always read and follow the herbicide label, and consider hiring a licensed applicator.

      Follow up: expect sprouts and seedlings for several seasons.

    5. Protect yourself

      Wear gloves. The sap can irritate skin.
